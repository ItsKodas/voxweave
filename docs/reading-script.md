# Reading script for training a voice

About 1,900 words: roughly 12 minutes at a relaxed pace, 10 if you read briskly, 15 if you take your time.

How to use it:

- Read in your normal speaking voice, at your normal distance from the microphone. Do not perform it.
- Pause for a breath between sentences. The app cuts the recording at those pauses.
- If you stumble, stop, breathe, and say the sentence again. A repeated sentence does no harm.
- Record it in one go with any recorder, then drop the file onto the Voices tab. Or read it a paragraph at a time with the Record button, keeping each take under 20 seconds.
- The sections change mood on purpose. Let your voice follow: plain for the plain parts, livelier for the questions and the excited parts, quieter for the calm ones.

## 1. Plain statements

The morning started the way most mornings do. I filled the kettle, found a clean cup, and stood by the window while the water boiled. Outside, a delivery van was blocking half the street, and two neighbours were having a cheerful argument about whose turn it was to move the bins.

My job is not complicated, but it does take patience. I check the figures, I answer the messages, and I try to finish one thing before starting the next. Some days that works. Other days, the phone rings just as I sit down, and the whole plan falls apart before nine o'clock.

The house we live in was built a long time ago. The floors creak, the doors stick in wet weather, and there is a cupboard under the stairs that nobody has opened in years. Even so, I would not swap it for anything newer. It is warm in winter, cool in summer, and quiet at night.

Every Thursday I walk to the market at the bottom of the hill. I buy bread, cheese, a bag of oranges, and whatever vegetables look fresh. The man who sells fish always asks about my family, and I always ask about his boat. Neither of us is in a hurry.

## 2. Questions

What time does the last train leave? Is it the one at ten past eleven, or has the timetable changed again? Could you check for me, please?

Have you ever noticed how quickly a week goes by? Where does the time go? One moment it is Monday morning, and the next you are wondering what to cook on Friday night.

Why did you choose the blue one? Was it cheaper, or did you simply like the colour? Would you pick the same one again if you had the choice?

Who left the back door open? Did anybody feed the cat? And whose shoes are these, sitting in the middle of the kitchen floor?

Do you think it will rain this afternoon? Should we take the umbrella, or risk it? How far is it to walk, anyway?

When you were a child, what did you want to be? A pilot? A vet? Somebody who drives a huge yellow digger? And when did you change your mind?

## 3. Excitement and surprise

You will never guess what happened! I opened the letter, read the first line, and nearly dropped my coffee. We won! After all these years of trying, we finally won!

Look at that! No, not there, higher up, just above the trees. Can you see it? What an incredible sight!

Wait a minute. That cannot be right. I counted them twice, and there were definitely eight. Now there are only seven. Somebody has eaten the last biscuit, and I think I know exactly who it was.

Oh, that is wonderful news! Congratulations to both of you! I am so happy I could shout it from the roof!

Stop! Do not touch that, it is still hot! Honestly, I turn my back for five seconds and this is what happens.

## 4. Calm and quiet

It is late now, and the street has gone still. The last bus went past an hour ago. A single light is on in the house across the road, and somewhere far away a dog is barking at nothing in particular.

Take a slow breath in, and let it out again. There is no rush. Whatever needed doing today is either done, or it will wait until tomorrow.

The lake was perfectly flat that evening. A thin mist lay over the water, and the hills on the far side had turned a soft shade of purple. We sat on the old wooden jetty and said very little, because there was nothing that needed saying.

Snow fell all night without a sound. By morning the garden had vanished under a thick white blanket, and even the birds seemed to be whispering.

## 5. Explaining something

To make a decent cup of tea, begin with fresh water. Bring it to the boil, warm the pot, and add one spoonful of leaves for each person. Pour the water over the leaves, wait for four minutes, and then pour the tea through a strainer. Add milk if you like it, sugar if you must, and drink it while it is hot.

Changing a bicycle tyre is easier than it looks. First, turn the bicycle upside down and loosen the wheel. Next, push the tyre levers under the edge of the tyre and work your way around the rim. Pull out the old tube, check the inside of the tyre for anything sharp, and fit the new tube before pumping it up.

If you get lost in a strange city, do not panic. Find a main road, look for a sign, and ask somebody who seems to know where they are going. Most people are glad to help, and a few will even walk you to the corner to be sure you take the right turning.

Here is how the game works. Each player takes five cards. The youngest player goes first. On your turn, you may swap one card, keep the cards you have, or challenge the player on your left. The first person to collect three matching pairs wins the round.

## 6. Numbers, dates and lists

There were twelve chairs, four tables, and one enormous sofa. The room measured six metres by nine, and the ceiling was nearly three metres high.

She was born on the fourteenth of March, nineteen eighty-seven, at twenty past six in the morning. She weighed just over three kilograms.

The recipe needs two hundred grams of flour, one hundred and fifty grams of butter, three eggs, half a teaspoon of salt, and a quarter of a litre of milk.

First we went to Paris, then to Vienna, then south to Rome, and finally home by way of Zurich. The whole journey took nineteen days and cost a little under two thousand pounds.

My telephone number ends in seven, seven, four, nine. The office is on the fifth floor, room five hundred and twelve, at the end of the corridor on the right.

Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, Sunday. January, February, March, April, May, June, July, August, September, October, November, December.

## 7. Conversation

"Are you coming with us tonight?" she asked.

"I am not sure yet," he said. "What time are you leaving?"

"About half past seven. We booked a table for eight."

"Then yes, probably. Let me finish this first. Should I bring anything?"

"Just yourself. And maybe a jacket, because it gets cold by the river."

"Fine. I will meet you outside. Do not go in without me!"

"We would never do that," she said, laughing. "Well, we might. So do not be late."

Later that evening, he admitted that he had enjoyed himself far more than he expected. "I almost stayed at home, you know," he said. "I am glad I changed my mind."

## 8. Opinions and feelings

I have never understood why people queue for hours to buy a new phone. The old one still works. The new one does almost exactly the same things, only slightly faster. Yet every year the line stretches around the block.

To be honest, I was disappointed. I had expected so much more. The reviews were glowing, the pictures looked beautiful, and the reality was a small grey room with a view of the car park.

There is nothing I enjoy more than a long walk with no particular destination. You notice things you would otherwise miss: a strange little shop, a garden full of sunflowers, a cat asleep on a warm wall.

I am sorry. I should have told you sooner, and I should not have raised my voice. It was unfair, and you deserved better from me.

Frankly, I think the whole plan is a mistake. It costs too much, it will take too long, and nobody has explained what happens if it goes wrong. I would rather say so now than keep quiet and regret it later.

## 9. Tricky sounds

The thirty-three thieves thought that they thrilled the throne throughout Thursday.

She sells sea shells by the sea shore, and the shells she sells are surely sea shells.

A big black bug bit a big black bear, and the big black bear bled badly.

Red lorry, yellow lorry. Red leather, yellow leather. Rural jurors rarely worry.

Fresh fried fish, five flat flasks, and a very fine view of the valley.

George judged the giant jug of ginger juice to be just a little too generous.

Which witch wished which wicked wish? Whether the weather is warm, or whether the weather is wet, we will weather the weather whatever the weather.

Six thick thistle sticks. Zebras zigzag through the busy zoo. The measure of pleasure is leisure and treasure.

## 10. A short story to finish

On the edge of the village there lived an old woman who repaired clocks. Her shop was no bigger than a garden shed, and every wall was covered with clocks of every shape and size. Some ticked loudly, some ticked softly, and one, in the corner, had not ticked at all for forty years.

One autumn afternoon, a boy came in out of the rain. He was carrying a small brass watch wrapped in a handkerchief. "It belonged to my grandfather," he explained. "It stopped on the day he died, and nobody has been able to start it since."

The old woman put on her glasses and opened the back of the watch. She looked for a long time without speaking. Then she picked up the smallest screwdriver the boy had ever seen, and she began to work.

"Will it take long?" the boy asked.

"That depends," she said. "Good things usually do. Sit down, and tell me about your grandfather."

So he told her. He told her about the fishing trips, the terrible jokes, and the smell of pipe smoke in the hallway. He told her how his grandfather could whistle any tune after hearing it once, and how he always kept a peppermint in his coat pocket for emergencies.

While he talked, the rain stopped. A thin line of sunlight crept across the workbench. And then, very quietly, the watch began to tick.

The boy stared at it. "How did you do that?"

The old woman smiled and closed the case. "I only cleaned it," she said. "You did the rest. A watch like this needs somebody to remember why it matters."

She would not take any money. The boy walked home through the wet, shining streets, holding the watch against his ear the whole way, and he never let it stop again.
